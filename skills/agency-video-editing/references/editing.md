# Editorial treatment and review

## Keep the speaker's meaning

Choose the most understandable complete take, then remove redundant takes, false
starts and genuinely empty pauses. Retain breaths, emotional pauses and context
that make the speech human or qualify a claim. Check joins by listening at normal
speed. Cover a distracting jump cut with relevant B-roll when available; do not
automatically punch in on an already tightly framed face.

A short social edit benefits from a clear new visual on changes of idea. Fast
education may use frequent cuts; serious news, demonstrations and readable evidence
often need longer holds. A prescribed number of cuts per second is not a quality
target. Preserve the user's reference pacing when given.

For news and factual claims, display the actual relevant source, highlight the words
being discussed and retain enough context to avoid changing their meaning. Verify
new factual material through the research route. Label illustrative reconstructions
so they cannot be mistaken for captured evidence. Do not insert unrelated memes
into a factual or sensitive story merely to make it busier.

## Captions and safe areas

Proofread the transcript against the audio, including names, units, amounts and
negations. Group short phrases by meaning; two or three words can suit a fast reel
but should not split a name or break comprehension. Use the brand's font and color,
high contrast and restrained styling. Correct transcription does not mean permission
to rewrite the speaker or automatically censor their words.

Treat safe areas as a delivery configuration, not a fixed property of 1080×1920.
Use the supplied platform template/reference or verify current placement guidance
when platform compliance is requested. Record the chosen caption rectangle and
reserved UI areas in the project. Keep important labels, faces and evidence clear
of them. Check the final scaled view and line wrapping, not just design coordinates.

## Layout by the material

| Material | Starting layout | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brief supporting image | Overlay near the speaker | Eyes, captions and meaningful gestures stay visible |
| Dense source, chart or code | Large panel or full screen | Relevant detail readable without misleading cropping |
| Vertical demo alongside speech | Split with roughly equal panels | Both subjects fill their panels without distortion |
| Landscape screen recording | Wider shallow panel plus speaker | Text survives final scale; enlarge the key region if necessary |
| Immersive B-roll or demonstration | Full-frame cutaway | It advances the actual point and returns coherently |

Pick layout before producing a motion asset. Crop deliberately; keep an entire
object only when that is necessary to understand it. A neutral background, intentional
letterboxing or a related blurred fill can all fit the brief. Avoid stretching media
or adding blur by reflex. Hold complex evidence long enough to read; move highlights
with the narration. A back-to-back asset change should not flash an empty frame.

## Audio

Listen before processing. Preserve the source voice; use noise reduction or voice
isolation only for a demonstrated problem and compare the result for artifacts.
Align sound effects to visible causes and keep them sparse. Music should support
the tone; serious news does not automatically call for suspense or conspiracy cues.
Use licensed/authorized files and retain their attribution requirements.

Measure peaks and, when a delivery specification calls for it, integrated loudness
and true peak using supported tooling. Adjust and remeasure after the final mix.
Do not claim a platform loudness target without checking its actual specification.

## Timelines and export

Store an explicit mapping from original clip times to output times. Recompute
captions, overlay intervals and sound cues after cuts or speed changes. Frame-accurate
cuts usually require decoding/re-encoding; stream-copy cuts can land at keyframes.
Normalize rotation, sample rate and time base intentionally before concatenating
incompatible clips. Do not silently change speed to meet a duration request.

Inspect the final encoded file, not only the preview. Check continuity, lip sync,
caption accuracy, crop, readability, audio joins and black/flash frames at every
boundary. Open representative extracted frames and listen to the mix. Export a
sidecar subtitle file when requested or useful for later edits. Delivery should
include enough source/EDL information to revise a caption or replace an asset
without reconstructing the entire edit.
