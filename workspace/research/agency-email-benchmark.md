# Agency Email Benchmark Notes

Source reviewed: `/home/gyanweshi/Downloads/Other Agencies - Email Content .docx`

## Agencies Reviewed

- Capsicum Mediaworks
- Fruit Bowl Digital
- SocialBeat
- Mindstorm
- Moshi Moshi
- Brandwitty
- TechMagnate
- Big Trunk Communications

## What The Emails Had In Common

Most agencies used the same broad sales motion:

- Acknowledge the inquiry.
- Avoid giving pricing or strategy immediately.
- Ask for a call, questionnaire, written brief, or more qualification details.
- Try to understand the client’s business before proposing scope.
- Move the lead toward one clear next step.

The shared intent was lead qualification. None of the agencies tried to close the client in the first email.

## Key Differences

### Capsicum Mediaworks

Capsicum used a proof-heavy SEO-first approach.

What stood out:

- Long client list.
- Case study links.
- Years of experience and global client count.
- SEO questionnaire as the next step.

Strength:
Strong credibility and proof.

Weakness:
Too long for a first email. The recipient has to work through a lot of information before reaching the next step.

### Fruit Bowl Digital

Fruit Bowl used a conversational, sales-led tone.

What stood out:

- Warm and direct.
- Mentions bespoke solutions.
- Pushes for a quick briefing call.
- Follow-ups feel human and persistent.

Strength:
Good tone and clear call orientation.

Weakness:
Less structured than Mindstorm or TechMagnate.

### SocialBeat

SocialBeat sent a formal acknowledgment.

What stood out:

- Says they will respond within 3 working days.
- Asks for a written brief.
- Shares profile, case studies, and YouTube links.

Strength:
Professional and organized.

Weakness:
Feels generic and less personalized.

### Mindstorm

Mindstorm had the strongest process-led email.

What stood out:

- Short positioning line: "A Strategic Growth Partner to ambitious brands."
- Clear discovery call as the next step.
- Explains what the call is designed to understand.
- Gives the recipient a direct scheduling path.
- Gives an alternate path: reply with a brief.
- Lists what the call will cover.
- Adds credibility in the signature, not the body.

Strength:
Best balance of professionalism, structure, and next-step clarity.

Weakness:
Some phrasing should not be copied directly because it can feel too close to their email.

### Moshi Moshi

Moshi Moshi used the most playful tone.

What stood out:

- Distinctive brand voice.
- Very casual.
- Direct phone number.

Strength:
Memorable personality.

Weakness:
Weak qualification, little proof, and not enough business context.

### Brandwitty

Brandwitty sent a generic acknowledgment.

What stood out:

- Polite and professional.
- Mentions they will review the inquiry.
- Gives a phone number for urgent assistance.

Strength:
Clear acknowledgement.

Weakness:
Feels automated and undifferentiated.

### TechMagnate

TechMagnate used the strongest qualification approach.

What stood out:

- ROI-focused positioning.
- Asks for SOW and budget.
- Later asks for decision-maker, target location, competitors, current agency challenges, and budget.

Strength:
Good business qualification.

Weakness:
Can feel more transactional and less creative-agency-like.

### Big Trunk Communications

Big Trunk focused only on reconnecting.

What stood out:

- Says they tried calling but could not connect.
- Asks for alternate number or convenient time.

Strength:
Simple and practical.

Weakness:
Misses the chance to position the agency or qualify the requirement.

## Best Elements To Borrow

Use these mechanics, not exact wording:

- From Mindstorm: structured next step, discovery call framing, scheduling path, alternate brief-sharing option.
- From TechMagnate: qualification discipline around scope, timelines, budget, decision process, and current challenges.
- From Fruit Bowl: natural professional-casual tone.
- From Capsicum: proof and credibility, but only when compressed.
- From Moshi Moshi: brand personality, but not at the cost of clarity.

## What To Avoid

- A long agency introduction.
- A full service dump in the first email.
- Large client lists in the body.
- Generic lines like "enhance your digital presence" without context.
- Saying "no pitch deck" in a cold or semi-warm email.
- Copying Mindstorm’s wording too closely.
- Starting with a process step and then awkwardly introducing the agency afterward.

## Verve Email Direction

The Verve version should not copy Mindstorm, but it should use the same underlying structure:

1. Thank the prospect for reaching out.
2. Add a short Verve positioning line.
3. Explain why a discovery call is the next step.
4. Offer a simple scheduling path.
5. Mention what the call will cover.
6. Give an alternate option to share a written brief.

The Verve positioning line should be short. It should not list every service.

Good direction:

> Verve Media helps brands shape clear creative communication across content, films, campaigns, and digital experiences.

Even tighter:

> Verve Media helps brands turn business goals into sharper creative, content, film, and digital communication.

## Recommended Verve Email Draft

Subject: Next steps for [Brand]

Hi [Name],

Thank you for getting in touch with Verve Media.

Verve Media helps brands shape clear creative communication across content, films, campaigns, and digital experiences. To understand where we can add value for [Brand], we’d first like to get clarity on your goals, current priorities, scope, and timelines.

Please let us know a convenient time for a short discovery call, and we’ll coordinate accordingly.

During the call, we can cover:

- Your brand and business context
- The problem you’re looking to solve
- Scope, timelines, and priorities
- Any references or expectations you already have in mind

If you already have a brief ready, feel free to share it over email. We’ll review it and shape the call around that.

Best,  
[Name]  
[Designation]  
Verve Media

## Working Principle

The email should feel professional-casual: warm, clear, and structured, but not robotic. The goal is not to sell Verve in the first email. The goal is to make the next step feel obvious and easy.
